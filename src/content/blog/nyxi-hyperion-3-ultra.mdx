## Feature Parity Is the Hard Part

There are plenty of Switch 2 Joy-Con alternatives out there at the moment, but not many offer parity with the full suite of features on Nintendo's originals. The NYXI Hyperion 3 Ultra does everything an original Joy-Con can do and, in a few places, goes well beyond it. Just so you know, this article (like the video it's based on) is sponsored by NYXI.

We'll get into the advanced features shortly, but the baseline for any pair of Joy-Con replacements has to be that they're more comfortable than the originals. The Hyperion 3 Ultra passes that test with flying colours.

## A Bigger, Bulkier Grip (In a Good Way)

I have larger hands and I climb a lot, so I really struggle when I come back to the original Switch 2 Joy-Cons. That slim, pebble-like design digs into my palms, and as nice as they are, you've heard it all before: they're just not comfortable for extended sessions.

The Hyperion 3 Ultra is an absolute beast in comparison. The grip is big and bulky, the plastic feels great, and a little bit of texture helps you hold on. They're the closest I've come to the Lenovo Legion Go S, which remains my most comfortable and ergonomically pleasing PC handheld. You get that same extra width and a spacious front section, and it genuinely feels like your Switch 2 has evolved into a PC handheld once they're clipped on. The grip moulds into the natural shape of your palms, and because everything is larger, you have ample access to every button.

The one exception is the home button, which is tucked into a corner that naturally sits beneath your hand. You have to pull your thumb right back to reach it, so if you're constantly fiddling with the brightness setting you'll notice. But it's rarely a gameplay button, so it hardly matters.

## What Matters Is How They Feel

The buttons themselves are where these controllers shine. To show you, I snapped them onto the little splitter, which turns the Joy-Cons into something closer to a traditional Bluetooth controller. It's far more comfortable than Nintendo's own splitter, which never feels particularly easy to use, especially when you're reaching for the buttons at the top. This feels like a proper controller you'd happily use for a long session on the couch.

NYXI markets this as a pro controller mode. It isn't quite as light or comfortable as the Switch 2 Pro Controller, which is one of the best controllers out there, but the Hyperion 3 Ultra differentiates itself in some key ways. There are micro switches under the ABXY buttons, and they have a really satisfying action. They aren't quite as clicky as some micro switches I've tried, so they're less noisy than you might expect, but I like a bit of audible feedback. The floating TMR sticks have just about the right level of tension too.

Then there's the metallic D-pad, which attaches magnetically and lifts straight off. You can swap in a shield-style D-pad that clips in the same way. I usually stick with the original style, but it's rather nice to sink your thumb into the shield, and it looks cool as well.

## Turbo, RGB and a Few Extras

There's HD rumble, and a set of function buttons lets you adjust vibration and the RGB lighting. I've set mine to mimic the blue and red of the original Switch 2 controllers. This is the brightest setting, and I do wish it could go a little higher because some people will find it too subtle, though I'm happy leaving it where it is. A turbo button lets you rapidly press A, which is handy for racing through dialogue in a game like _Pokémon_.

The two rear paddles can be mapped as well, with the same metallic paint effect and micro switches underneath the triggers. On the practical side, the splitter has a USB-C port for charging both controllers, and each controller has its own USB-C port for firmware updates or charging separately. The release button works well too: press in and the controllers pull away from the splitter.

## Mouse Mode, _Mario Kart_ and Wrist Straps

With the controllers detached, you can snap on the little mouse mode adapters. There's a sensor on the bottom of the controller, so mouse mode works, and it's much more comfortable than using the original Switch 2 Joy-Con. I'm not sure how many people use mouse mode, but I suspect that's partly because the originals don't feel great as a mouse. Here you get left and right click, and you can use your finger on the back paddle, so remapping becomes genuinely useful. A mouse resting on a vertical axis will always wobble a little, but it's impressive that NYXI have recreated Switch 2's mouse mode at all.

The controllers also work in a _Mario Kart_ style with the shoulder buttons, and adding the wrist strap means you can play something like _Wii Sports Resort_ without the controller flying towards your TV. They even work with amiibos. It's this full feature parity that sets the Hyperion 3 Ultra apart, and I think it's finally time we stopped relying on the original Joy-Cons as the default option for the Switch 2.
