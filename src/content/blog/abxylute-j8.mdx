## Big Hands, Small Joy-Cons

Over the past week, two things have been very difficult to remove from my Switch 2: _Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave_ and the <a href="https://abxylute.com/products/switch-2-j8-controller?sca_ref=12708878.2OAsvctopC" target="_blank" rel="sponsored nofollow">abxylute J8</a>. You might think a controller needs a fighting game or a punishing platformer to be tested properly, but my time with that strategy game shows both what the J8 does really well and where I think it could improve.

First, comfort. Lay the Joy-Con 2 next to the J8 and the difference in size and ergonomics is obvious. I still think the Joy-Con 2 is the more aesthetically pleasing option, with its pebble-like design and a lot of tech packed inside. But if you have large adult hands, they can lead to cramp after a while, and that edge has a habit of digging into your palms.

The J8s are far kinder to bigger hands like mine. I recently took up bouldering, so my hands are often under quite a lot of duress, and coming home to these rather than the tiny Joy-Cons feels much better. Part of that is the larger surface area and bigger buttons, but turn them around and you will also find a rounded grip that fits the palm nicely, with a bit of texture to help you hold on. I can play for much longer without discomfort, and I can hold them in a wider variety of positions, whether I'm lying on the bed or sitting up on the sofa.

## Detached Duty

That grip shape makes the J8s more comfortable when they are snapped onto the console, but they are a little harder to wield as standalone controllers. The larger buttons and slightly different layout are part of it. The D-pad sits a bit lower than on the original Switch, so you find yourself reaching down for it, and you naturally gravitate towards the joystick instead.

Even so, they keep a surprising amount of parity with the Joy-Cons. You don't get mouse mode, which I doubt will be a huge miss for most people, but the pins and magnetic connection mean you can take the wrist strap from your original Joy-Cons and attach it to the J8. That makes four-player _Mario Kart_ perfectly doable, and the shoulder buttons work exactly as you would expect. It is much easier to control than the dinky little Joy-Con, and the strap means you can play _Wii Sports Resort_ without worrying about launching a controller at your TV.

Detached, you also get access to the back button, which could be far more comfortable for item usage or grinding on rails in _Mario Kart World_. The back buttons are programmable and can be set to macros through an app.

Attaching them to the Switch 2 works as you would expect from any Joy-Con, complete with the nice little animation, and a trigger makes them easy to remove. Stability is the one caveat. They are not quite as secure as the official Joy-Cons, and if you flop them around a lot they will come off. In normal use that has never happened to me, and it takes some deliberate wobbling to get them off, but if you are the sort of person who holds your Switch by one side, it is worth knowing.

## RGB, Faceplates and Rumble

On the back are two small light buttons that control the RGB effects. Hold one and press up or down to change the brightness, or left and right to change the colour. A single press cycles through the modes: a rainbow effect, single colours, and a pulsing single colour. If you miss that splash of colour from the Joy-Cons, the J8 has you covered, and I think it looks like a nice addition.

There is also a magnetic faceplate that snaps off. I have seen these on quite a few controllers, and none of those manufacturers has ever released custom plates. I live in hope but remain sceptical, though I would love to be proven wrong if abxylute does release alternative designs.

Rumble is where the J8 falls short. There is no HD rumble, and it is nowhere near as good as the Joy-Cons. In _Fire Emblem_ I resorted to switching it off, because it feels hollow and is really loud in comparison. If you want to adjust the intensity, hold the T button and go up or down, but hold it for too long and it will mess up your light setting and actually reset the controller. If you care a lot about rumble, this is not the controller for you.

## Micro-Switch Heaven

If you are a fan of mechanical switches behind your buttons, though, this controller is a dream. The ABXY buttons are a little taller, glossier and rounder than on the original Switch, and so much more comfortable to press, with a lovely clicky sound as they hit the micro-switches.

The challenge with any controller like this is that those buttons have to work as face buttons when you hold it sideways, and as a traditional D-pad when it is attached to the console. I'm not claiming this is the best option for anyone deep into _Smash Bros._ or fighting games. But for _Fire Emblem_, I have found it really good. You have to be precise about which square you are selecting, and the audible feedback plus a very sensitive D-pad has made playing on hard classic mode a little less stressful.

You also get two capture buttons, one on each side, along with home, plus and minus. The shoulder buttons and triggers use micro-switches too. They are very clicky and, because this is still a Switch controller, they don't travel far, but for a game like _Fire Emblem_ it is a brilliant set to have.

## Final Thoughts

All in all, I think these are a superb set of controllers. Until now I would have said the dbrand Killswitch grips were the most comfortable way for me to play the Switch portably, but the J8s have replaced them, with the bonus that you can slide them onto your Switch dock without taking them off. They can pretty much live on the console from now on. The Joy-Cons, realistically, will end up inside steering wheel accessories for whenever my son wants to play _Mario Kart_.

I will be reviewing some other Switch controllers very soon, so subscribe to the channel if you want to see whether any of them can budge me from the J8. As things stand, this is my go-to Switch setup.

I almost forgot to mention the carry case that comes in the box, which is actually really nice. It is made from hard plastic, you simply slide the controllers in, and there are spots for carrying a few game cartridges too. It is a handy extra if you want to take your Switch on the go.

**My recommendation:** If you have larger hands and want a more comfortable, clickier alternative to the Joy-Con 2, the abxylute J8 is an easy yes. Just don't buy it if HD rumble matters to you.
